# 2050–2080 Global Working-Age Population: Three Scenarios × Long-Wave Model (Nonlinear) Forecast

**Author**: sacanlee | **Date**: 2026-08-23. Script: `scripts/forecast_2050_2080.py`; data: `data/forecast_results.json`.

## 1. Task and method

Construct three scenarios (optimistic/medium/pessimistic) for the 2050–2080
growth rate of the global working-age population (15–64) from the **latest
authoritative population projections**, feed the resulting n into the long-wave
model (book Eqs. 8.15–8.19, book parameter values), and compute the period and
the rise/fall segment lengths and shares with the **nonlinear method** (direct
numerical integration of the original equations, 6T window, multi-cycle median).

## 5. Nonlinear-method forecast results

For each n: closed-form equilibrium → analytical Jacobian → eigenvalues; then
nonlinear integration of the original equations with 5% perturbation, 6T window,
peak/trough detection with multi-cycle medians (**sC(t) is the reliable measure —
for positive n, r(t) is drift-polluted and reference only**).

| Scenario | n | Equilibrium τ\* | Equilibrium r\* | T (eigenvalue) | Nonlinear sC: T | Nonlinear sC rise/fall (share) | Nonlinear r (ref.) rise/fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic** | +0.464% | 0.0554 | 0.258 | **35.3 y** | **38.9 y** | 20.0 y (50.2%) / 19.8 y (49.8%) | 9.3 (19%) / 40.0 (81%) |

## 6. Interpretation (key points)

1. **Period**: the 2050–2080 long-wave dominant period is **31–35 y** (nonlinear
   34–39 y; linear eigenvalue 31.0–35.3 y), a shortening of about **20–25%**
   relative to the active Wave 5 (1982–2022, measured n(15–64) = 1.62% → model
   42.6 y); ~20% relative to the book calibration (n=0.015 → 41.7 y). Progressive:
   as labour-force growth slides in 2045–70, the model rhythm accelerates; after
   2070 (negative n) it enters the 31–32-year tier.
2. **Rise/fall shares**: almost symmetric (49.4%–50.2% rise); **the pessimistic
   tier starts to invert** (fall 50.6%); compared with deeper negative n
   (−0.8%/−1.5%) where the fall share is 56–68%, the 2050–80 asymmetry is still
   shallow, but **the direction is set**: later and more pessimistic → more
   downswing-heavy.
3. **Mechanism (built into the model)**: τ\* (rate of technical change) =
   0.055–0.063 across the three scenarios (above the W5-era 0.044–0.046):
   **stronger labour scarcity → faster mechanisation/automation → faster
   fluctuation**; equilibrium profit rate r\* 0.26→0.34 (inflated), sV\*
   0.075–0.11 (shrinking labour-investment share); divergence coefficient
   +0.028→+0.035, larger amplitude amplification.
4. **Revision of earlier reports**: the earlier 28.4/24.9 y for n = −0.008/−0.015
   were **deep scenarios**; UN WPP 2024 medium shows 2050–80 global 15–64 turns
   negative only mildly (overall +0.048%), so **the most likely current case is
   ~33 y for the medium tier and ~31 y for the pessimistic tier**; deep shortening
   (25–28 y) is more likely in 2085–2100 (under the medium scenario total
   population also starts falling after 2084; combined with the Low variant and
   the Lancet/IHME reference scenario, negative-n depth would increase).
5. **Uncertainty**: the variant approximation is conservative (the pessimistic
   value is actually an optimistic-leaning lower bound); model linearisation and
   printed inconsistencies are documented in earlier reports; this table is an
   order-of-magnitude forecast — the direction and magnitude (30–35 y, rise/fall
   half-split, gradually heavier downswing) are robust.

## 7. Wave 6 timeline (2030 as trough)

**Premise**: the crisis of Wave 5 (from 2007) extends into the 2020s; assume
**2030 is the trough/start of Wave 6**; n sequence: the 2030–2040 working-age
cohorts **were already born before 2039 — the growth is "already determined"**
(UN WPP 2024 medium actual: 2030–2040 CAGR = **+0.55%**, yearly rates
+0.77%→+0.43%); the 2040–2070 segment uses the **earlier medium forecast**
(2045–2070 CAGR = **+0.16%**). Wave 6 is thus split into two segments:

| Segment | Period | n (CAGR, 15–64) | Model T (eigenvalue) | Nonlinear sC: T | Rise share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upswing | 2030–2045 | **+0.49%** | 35.4 y | 39.2 y | 50.3% |
| Downswing | 2045–2070 | **+0.16%** | 33.5 y | 37.6 y | 50.2% (fall 49.8%) |

**Wave 6 timeline (main measure: eigenvalue period + nonlinear rise share)**:

| Event | Time | Computation |
|---|---|---|
| Trough (start) | **2030** | scenario premise (tail of the Wave 5 crisis) |
| **Peak (boom top / crisis onset)** | **≈ 2048–2050** | 2030 + rise ≈ 2030 + 0.503×35.4 (≈17.8 y) → 2048; with the nonlinear T ≈ 2050 |
| **Trough (crisis bottom)** | **≈ 2065–2068** | peak + fall (≈0.498×33.5 ≈ 16.7 y; nonlinear-T measure 18.7 y) |
| **Wave 6 total period** | **≈ 34.5–38.4 y** | 17.8 + 16.7 (eigenvalue measure) ≈ 34.5; nonlinear sC ≈ 38.4 |
| Next trough | ≈ 2065–2068 | closure of the full long wave from 2030 |

**Three noteworthy intersections**:

1. **Coupling with the labour-force peak**: the model puts the Wave 6 trough at
   2065–2068, while the UN WPP 2024 medium global 15–64 peak is exactly **2070**,
   turning negative after — the wave's "crisis bottom" nearly coincides with the
   "global working-age peak" (±2–5 y): **when labour supply actually starts to
   contract is exactly when the long wave enters deep crisis**, structural
   evidence for "demographic factors dominating Wave 6".
2. **Historical-bias correction of the model system**: the model underestimates
   the phase length of modern waves (W4/W5) by 8–15pp (e.g. W5's actual rise
   26 y vs the model's 21 y), so the peak carries an ~5-year early bias;
   a more realistic calibrated reading: **peak ≈ 2051–2055, trough ≈ 2068–2073**.
3. **Consistency with earlier forecasts**: Wave 6's total period 34.5–38.4 y
   falls in the middle of the §5 three-scenario range (31–39 y); the average
   rhythm is ~10–15% faster than Wave 5 (≈40 y), rise/fall nearly symmetric with
   a slightly increasing fall share (49.8%→50.6% in the deep pessimistic tier),
   consistent with the long-run judgment "faster fluctuation, gradually heavier
   downswing".

## 7.1 Wave 7 (from 2070, UN medium extension)

**Link**: the Wave 6 trough (model 2065–2068, coinciding with the UN 15–64 peak
of 2070) is the start of Wave 7; take **2070 as the start year** (UN medium
15–64 turns negative after that, covering fully to 2100).

**Wave 7 phase n (15–64, UN medium data)**: 2070–2085 = **−0.137%**/yr (upswing
segment), 2085–2100 = **−0.103%**/yr (downswing segment); full wave (2070–2100)
−0.120%/yr. 15–64 declines gently from 6.286bn (2070) to 6.064bn (2100) —
**much milder than the deep scenarios** (global population inertia buffers).

| Model | Rise (share) | **Peak** | Fall | **Total period** | Trough |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Eigenvalue measure | 15.8 y (49.7%) | **≈2086** | 16.1 y | **32.0 y** | ≈2102 |
| Nonlinear sC measure | 17.4 y (49.7%) | **≈2087** | 17.8 y | **35.2 y** | ≈2105 |

**Key points**:
1. Wave 7 total period **32–35 y**, a further ~8% below Wave 6 (34.5–38.4 y) —
   **the long-wave rhythm accelerates wave by wave**;
2. Rise/fall nearly symmetric (rise share 49.7%), but the fall segment remains
   slightly dominant (nonlinear sC 50.3% down; r measure 84% down) — the
   "downswing-heavy" character of the post-demographic era continues;
3. **Structural coincidence**: the Wave 7 peak (≈2086–2087) coincides with the
   UN medium **global total-population peak (2084, ≈10.3bn)**; after the peak,
   total population and working-age population contract in synchrony, so Wave 7's
   downswing is driven by a "double contraction" (population + labour force),
   theoretically harder to support with demographic tailwinds than Wave 6 (where
   labour force merely flattened);
4. Given the model's systematic 8–15% underestimation of 20th-century wave
   phases, add a 5-year safety margin to peak/trough years: **total period
   32–40 y, peak 2086–2092, trough 2102–2112**;
5. **Cross-reference**: under the "trough-to-trough" chain (Wave 6 trough
   ≈2063–2065), Wave 7 starts in **2063** (UN baseline) rather than 2070 — the
   period is unchanged (32.0–32.5 y), **the peak shifts to ≈2079–2086 and the
   trough to ≈2095–2102**; the full re-estimation under the SSA fertility-crash
   scenario is in `ssa_fertility_scenario.md` (Wave 6 2030→2048→2063, 33.5 y;
   Wave 7 2063→2079→2095, 32.5 y).
